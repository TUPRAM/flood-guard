## Ten-minute speaking script

The following is spoken copy for a live demonstration. It deliberately omits numerical claims while the new package is being frozen; add counts only after matching the Public projection, Planning view, Studio report and regenerated brief to one exact release. The time windows include clicks and deliberate pauses, so rehearse them with the actual Preview and offline backup.

### 0:00-0:45 · What the product can say

"FloodGuard turns a possible flood observation into a question a local planner can investigate: which residents and services might be affected, and which road assumptions matter most? I am showing a non-operational research case. It is not an official warning, a live road report or an instruction to evacuate. The three views serve different readers, but they point to the same case and evidence record. I will begin with the public view, follow one consequence into Planning, and then show how Studio lets us audit it."

### 0:45-1:45 · Place, time and denominator

"This is Mae Sai core and the September 2024 study event. The search area on this map is not a whole subdistrict. Population figures are modelled residents inside the relevant area intersection, using their stated source year. The satellite question is narrower than the entire flood event: it compares a 3 September pre-acquisition with a 15 September post-acquisition in UTC, which are 4 and 16 September in Thailand. A feature outside the valid satellite footprint, or at another point in the flood, cannot be inferred from this pair. The public case shows the event window, source time and evidence status separately so the date of a source is not confused with the date of an impact."

### 1:45-3:00 · Method and GeoAI boundary

"The physical processing path starts with the original Sentinel-1 SAFE data. The documented SNAP graph calibrates Beta0, terrain-flattens it to Gamma0, and aligns the pre- and post-images to a 10-metre analysis grid with valid-data and exclusion masks. That is a specific processing choice; the proposal's literal Sigma0 and speckle wording does not describe this run. A deterministic threshold is then a candidate detector, not a trained AI model or a calibrated flood probability. In the conservative adaptive-threshold diagnostic, all 81 local windows abstained, which is a result we report rather than hide. The previously published amplitude-change candidate remains separately labelled and unvalidated. Independent September reference material, purpose rights, frozen evaluation and downstream acceptance are still needed before any observation or FPPS result can be called accepted."

### 3:00-4:30 · One local consequence

"Now I keep the same Mae Sai event and select hospital access on foot in Planning. This is a service-specific denominator of modelled residents, with a separately labelled baseline 30-minute access count. When the candidate flood intersection is used to impose road closures, the view separates people who lose the 30-minute threshold from people who become newly unreachable in the graph. Those outcomes answer different questions and must not be added. The road intersections are assumptions, not observed closures, and the hospital sites are mapped candidates, not verified open facilities. Unknown connectors and people without a route remain visible instead of being treated as zero impact. Read the exact release-bound counts from the screen and brief only after the freeze."

### 4:30-5:45 · Inspect the decision lever

"Here is one prepared route comparison. It starts from a named origin and shows the model's before and after path when a declared link or destination is removed. One route does not represent every resident in the population statistic. The useful action today is to check the high-consequence road link, its bridge or grade context, and the hospital's real entrance and operation against dated local information. Then compare a plausible open-link alternative. This tells a reviewer where to spend verification effort; it does not certify that the displayed route is passable or safe. The brief records that same next check in English and Thai, with the denominator and assumptions alongside it."

### 5:45-6:45 · Transfer and replication

"FloodGuard also keeps the other study areas separate. Hat Yai has its own November 2025 candidate source and an urban closure sensitivity, but it has not borrowed Mae Sai accuracy or gained independent event validation from it. A lower Chao Phraya research run evaluates a wider, documented routing context while holding the original demand area and reporting units fixed. The 2024 and 2025 case views can share static access context, yet they lack separately qualified flood observations for those years. A difference between event labels is therefore not a measured year-on-year change in flood access. Each area's status appears with its own evidence, rather than one national confidence label."

### 6:45-7:45 · What is missing from an accepted priority

"The five FPPS components retain their fixed 30, 25, 20, 15 and 10 percent default weights. We do not insert zero for a missing component or change its weight to make the score complete. Calibrated flood likelihood, compatible exposure and vulnerability inputs, reviewed access consequences, and a separate downstream acceptance decision are not yet in place. The accepted FPPS and A-to-E action class therefore remain unavailable in this release. Class E means monitor and verify; it never means safe. Children mean ages 0 through 14. A local modelled age sensitivity exists, but its 2024 age surface is combined with 2020 demand and later routing, so it cannot be presented as measured event-day age exposure or accepted equity. Real shelter capacity and operating status are also unknown."

### 7:45-8:45 · Audit and communication

"Studio lets an analyst follow the same case into the method and evidence details: source identity and rights, processing masks, footprints, selected scenario, route and population accounts, uncertainty, and the human gates that remain closed. The downloaded brief and the on-screen numbers are generated from the same pinned case package. The Public view gives a short preparedness reading; Planning makes the service and route assumptions explicit; Studio carries the audit trail. English and Thai versions keep the same evidence status and numbers. An offline package is prepared from the release for rehearsal when network access is unavailable, but it is still a research snapshot, not a live feed."

### 8:45-10:00 · Decision and close

"The immediate decision I recommend is a verification sequence, not an evacuation order: confirm a dated Mae Sai flood footprint, inspect the road link and facility entrance most consequential to modelled access, and check the population denominator and any unsupported area. Independent reviewers can then decide whether the reference and evaluation are fit for the stated purpose. Only after those gates, and a separate downstream decision acceptance, should an accepted priority and action class be recomputed. What FloodGuard already improves is traceability: a public explanation, a planner's comparison and a scientific audit refer to the same bounded case. What remains incomplete is the promised qualified observation-to-priority chain. The final release links, briefs and offline package identify exactly what was tested and what still needs human evidence."

## Standalone limitations slide

**Title: What this release supports, and what it does not yet establish**

- **Candidate observation:** the 15 September comparison covers a specific satellite acquisition and valid footprint. The adaptive Otsu diagnostic abstained in 81 of 81 windows; the separate amplitude-change candidate is unvalidated.
- **Assumed consequences:** flood intersection imposes road closures in the model. Bridge passability, facility entrances and operation, usable shelter capacity and current conditions are unverified.
- **Modelled residents:** WorldPop counts and source years describe a residential context, not victims, event-day presence or whole-subdistrict totals. The local age/access sensitivity mixes years and is not accepted equity.
- **Decision gate:** no accepted FPPS or A-E action class is published. Independent reference/evaluation, five-event learned-model custody where applicable, and separate downstream decision acceptance remain open. Class E never means safe.
- **Safe use now:** use the case to choose what to verify next; confirm dated flood, road, service and population evidence with responsible reviewers before operational decisions.
