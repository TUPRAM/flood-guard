# outputs/planning_v1

Small committed outputs of the planning overlay (restructuring plan v2, section 3.1). The first table lists the receipts of the engineering runs made for protocol v1b before it was signed, including the run of record of the corridor and the E4 build of record. None of those files holds an FPPS, an A–E class, an ensemble or a value for a single tambon. Protocol v1b was signed and recorded on 3 October 2026 (`docs/proposal_execution/RECEIPTS.jsonl`); the runs made since then are in the second table, under "Runs made with protocol v1b in force". The owners answered every owner choice on 3 October 2026 (decision log R12); the status column says which receipt that turned into evidence of record. A review the same day reopened OI-04 and OI-06 of protocol v1b: the join log and the facility counts of the run of record were measured by the E0 spike, and those two items take their values from task E4 (owner-choices entry 1, plan row G7b). In R13 the owners chose to wait for E4 (entry 24, option A); the E4 build of record reproduced the spike's values exactly, and both items closed from it. A file written before R12 is not rewritten, so its own `status` field may still say `candidate`; protocol v1b says what it is now. The planning-frame build of 3 October names tambons, so its receipt sits beside the frames in `resources/planning_frames/`, not here.

**Every file** carries `generated_at_utc`, `source_timestamp`, `confidence_class` (always `low`), `confidence_basis`, `assumptions`, `official_warning: false` and `operational_status: non_operational`. In a JSON file they are at the top level; in a GeoJSON file they are in the feature's properties. A candidate file also carries `status: candidate` inside the file, and a file of the run of record carries `status: of_record` (`log_of_record` for the join log, `run_of_record` for the receipt), so a copy taken out of this folder still says what it is. `tests/test_planning_v1_outputs.py` checks all of this for every file here. None of these outputs is an official warning or an observation of a flood.

Each spike file carries its generation time, so its SHA-256 changes from run to run. To compare two runs, use the hashes of the content alone: `geometry_sha256` in a corridor file, `joins_sha256` in a join log and `context.canonical_sha256` in a spike receipt. The `reproducibility` block of a spike receipt does that comparison against the run before it.

The inputs are read-only files outside Git. Their locations are arguments of each script, and their SHA-256 values are in each receipt. The owner decisions are in `docs/proposal_execution/planning_protocol_v1b_owner_choices.md`, answered on 3 October 2026 as recommended (decision log R12).

A run that replaces an earlier one needs `--supersede "<reason>"`. **What the ranking measured, for the whole frame.** 26,130 of the 65,673 baseline edges (65,328 road edges and 345 grade-join connectors) carry a baseline route. 99.6% of the residents with a route reach a main-road entry before a hospital, so trunk and primary edges carry no flow and are not ranked. The 20 highest-ranked links are 20 consecutive edges of one tertiary street (OSM way 1114870832), with 8,722 to 9,261 residents each. The top 200 lie on five OSM ways and include two grade-join connectors (ranks 163 and 164).

**What the protocol leaves open** (`open_points` in the receipt; nothing below is decided by the script):

- **E6-OP5, graph bridges.** The rule says "Add graph bridges (Tarjan) to cover the all-routes-lost case" and not what they are added to (the ranked set, the 200 edges of the full reroute or the 20 of the output) or where a bridge stands against a link ranked by flow. Nothing is added: a bridge is flagged and ranked by its flow. The graph has 23,311 graph bridges and 8,317 of them are ranked; none is among the top 200, and the best-ranked one is at rank 1,473 with 371 residents. Removing an edge that is not a graph bridge leaves every node connected, so closing any single top-20 link on its own isolates nobody. Class rule v2 trigger B closes on its own a top-20 link that intersects the flood extent and counts the residents who lose every route, so it can only fire through a top-20 link that is a graph bridge.
- **E6-OP1, grade-join connectors.** The protocol ranks edges and does not say whether a connector can be a critical link. 168 connectors are ranked. 163 of them carry exactly the flow of a road edge and are ranked ahead of it, because a connector ID sorts before a road edge ID (90 of them directly ahead). The run stops for a connector among the top 20; there is none. In one of the 13 units that hold a ranked link, a connector is among the three highest-ranked links of the unit, which scenario S3 reads from the same table.
- **E6-OP6, a later run of the ranking.** The protocol does not say whether a run that supersedes the first run may change the recorded ranking. An answer to E6-OP1 or E6-OP5 may change the table, so both are needed before a later task uses its SHA-256.
- **E6-OP7, a zero-minute edge between two destinations.** The route is carried across it to the destination with the smaller ID, as the comparison of `select_interventions` does. 40 destination nodes are routed onward this way, and they carry no resident.
- **E6-OP2, E6-OP3, E6-OP4.** What the full reroute of the top 200 writes (none was run); whether the bridge flag is the graph bridge or the OSM tag (both are written); the unit of a link whose midpoint lies in no unit or in more than one (none occurred).

**How the shares were computed.** Counts are allocated to a tambon by the projected-area fraction of each 1 km cell inside it, as protocol v1b states for the national anchors (`national_vulnerability_anchors.method.allocation`, drafter reading DR-B04). The run uses the functions that produced the anchors (`floodguard.evidence_age_surface.summarize_geometry` and the readers of `scripts/build_national_vulnerability_anchors.py`) and the same input bytes. **What the run refuses.** The anchor receipt records the SHA-256 of three whole files. Protocol v1b builds the rest of planning frame v1 in one of them (`normalisation.py`), so a whole-file comparison would refuse every run made after that work merges. The builder binds what the share depends on: the source lines of `dependent_share`, `summarize_geometry`, `_coverage`, `read_units`, `check_age_sources` and `sha256_file`, and the values of the age-band and boundary-layer constants, pinned in `ANCHOR_RUN_CODE` from files that had the SHA-256 the anchor receipt records. A run is refused when one of them, or an input, differs. A whole file that has changed elsewhere is recorded in the receipt (`whole_file_same`) and does not refuse the run. `tests/test_age_exposure.py` runs the same check on the checked-out code, so a merge that changes one of those functions fails in the suite and not at run time. **What low and high are.** The plan gives a range for residents inside a flood input, [known, known + unsupported] (plan 1.3, MVP-02), and no formula for how the 1 km cell size moves a tambon's share. **What the runs show.** Over the frame (305.56 km²) the candidate area is 9.42 km² for the UN-SPIDER reproduction, 114.50 km² for M1-literal and 3.01 km² for M1-v2. M1-v2 gave no answer for six of the nine tiles, 77.3% of the frame's cells, so it does not meet the T2 skill bar at Mae Sai (abstention at most 0.20); read tambon by tambon the Mae Sai conditions pass in Ban Dai only. The GEOID result behind the fourth condition (0.411) is not distinguishable from 0.40 on 14 tiles, 67.9% of the GEOID test cells had no answer, and it measures agreement with a same-pass CEMS map, not an independent check (decision log R15).

**The layers of the run of record are displaced by about 680 m** (670 m west and 130 m north, measured on the pre-event image against mapped permanent water). Two things cause it and add up. The control points of the product sit at the heights of a coarse terrain model: 685 m interpolated over the frame (median), where the median height of the frame is 382 m. And the polynomial that GDAL fits to the control points does not pass through them: the warp asks for no order, GDAL chooses the second order (not an affine fit, although the plan's label says "GCP affine": A4-OP7), and over the frame that polynomial reads the image 261 m nearer to the sensor (median) than a spline through every control point. The height part is 414 m (median) and the two mappings lie 713 m apart (median); the parts add up cell by cell, the medians need not. The sensitivity run leaves about 10 m. Which layers case O1 uses is an owner decision (`open_points`, A4-OP1); until then the layers of the run of record are for tambon totals only.

**The two images of the run of record are offset from each other by about 9 m.** Each date is warped with its own polynomial. Measured on the images, the pre-event content lies 8.8 m east and 1.5 m south of the post-event content (an estimate to a fraction of a cell; a cell is 10 m); in the sensitivity run the same measurement gives 0.1 m. All three methods compare the two dates cell by cell, so the offset adds change that is not on the ground wherever the backscatter has an edge. What that does to the candidates was not measured. The replay's Sentinel-1 layers are made with the same warp (`scripts/build_mae_sai_flood_timeline.py`); they were not measured and the replay was not changed here (A4-OP8).

**The +1 dB level of M1-literal is cut at 0 dB.** M1-literal flags a cell only where delta-VH is below the threshold and below 0 dB, and the run keeps that clause at every level. Only two of the nine tiles get the full shift of +1 dB; in five the loosest level is the central level again. The band of 55.81, 114.50 and 131.62 km² is narrower above the central level by construction (`threshold_levels.upper_level`, A4-OP9). The minus and plus one-pixel levels that protocol v1b gives the UN-SPIDER reproduction are not written (`not_computed`, A2-OP2).
